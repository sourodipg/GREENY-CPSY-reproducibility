# Literature and design basis

The experiment uses established dyadic/network distinctions rather than inventing an actor/partner vocabulary for a symmetric event.

1. Cook & Dreyer (1984), *The Social Relations Model: A New Approach to the Analysis of Family-Dyadic Interaction*, Journal of Marriage and Family 46:679-693. The SRM separates actor, partner and relationship effects in dyadic data. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/352547.
2. An, Jager, Putnick & Bornstein (2023), *Parenting stress and attachment insecurity in young adulthood: A social relations model*, Journal of Marriage and Family 85:556-579. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12893. The paper explicitly separates actor, partner, relationship and family components and reports dyadic reciprocity.
3. Snijders (2017), *Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models for Network Dynamics*, Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application 4:343-363. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-statistics-060116-054035. Dynamic network analysis commonly treats reciprocity, transitivity, actor covariates and dyadic covariates as distinct structural effects.
4. Yang & Nelson (1991), *Using Common Random Numbers and Control Variates in Multiple-Comparison Procedures*, Operations Research 39:583-591. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.39.4.583. Common random numbers are used here to reduce variance in paired stochastic-simulation comparisons.

Design implication: the primary contrast is a matched directed-versus-reciprocal update on the *same unordered encounter ledger*. A separate GREENY reference is reported for context. Because GREENY does not contain adaptive edge formation, community detection is not treated as a causal mechanism; the informative network quantities are encounter reciprocity, repeat-pair recurrence, pair-state coupling, and local concentration statistics.
