\subsection*{Literature-grounded contact-network audit}
The recurrent-local condition is analysed as a temporal contact network rather than as an emergent social network. A contact is represented as a time-stamped pair $(i,j,t)$ following the contact-sequence formulation of Holme and Saram\"aki. The time-aggregated exposure graph places an undirected edge between agents who meet at least once and assigns edge weight $w_{ij}$ equal to the number of contacts between them. Node degree is the number of distinct neighbours and node strength is the sum of incident edge weights, following standard weighted-network terminology. Scalar attribute assortativity is computed from the correlation of attribute values at the two ends of contact edges, following Newman; a second version weights each edge by $w_{ij}$ to retain repeated-contact information.

For the frozen M kernel and even $N$, cyclic shifts differing by two produce the same unordered perfect matching. The union of the two shift-parity matchings is therefore a cycle through the agents sorted by the exogenous coordinate $z$. This graph identity is verified from the frozen kernel and is treated as an implementation invariant, not as a social-network claim. Because the M contact process is exogenous, edge weight is interpreted as repeated imposed exposure rather than relationship strength or natural attachment.
