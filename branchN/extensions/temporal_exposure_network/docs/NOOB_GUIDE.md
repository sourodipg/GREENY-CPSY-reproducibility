# Noob-proof guide

## What is being graphed?

Not friendship.

Not natural attachment.

The graph is simply a map of **who the frozen experiment forced to interact with whom**.

## Three levels

1. **Temporal contacts:** `(person i, person j, time t)` — who met whom and when.
2. **Exposure graph:** connect two people if they ever met; weight the edge by how many times they met.
3. **Trait-labelled graph:** put anxiety and avoidance on the nodes and ask whether highly repeated contacts tend to connect similar traits.

## Why this matters for BranchN

BranchN already showed that all coordinate realizations have essentially the same recurrence:

`repeat1 ≈ 0.5002`, `repeat5 ≈ 0.9692`, `repeat20 ≈ 0.9998`.

Yet the M one-sided-to-two-sided effect changes across coordinate realizations.

The graph audit asks the next simple question:

> Does the effect change because different people were placed next to different people?

## What counts as a useful result?

A useful result would look like:

`graph property changes across coordinate realizations`

and

`the same graph property tracks the M effect across those realizations`.

That would identify a plausible structural route.

A null result would also be useful: if ordinary graph/trait-mixing measures do not explain the BranchN sensitivity, attention moves to trajectory-level dynamics or temporal ordering.

## What must not be said

Do not write “the agents formed a social network.”

Do not write “the agents became attached because they repeatedly met.”

The correct description is **imposed recurrent exposure** and **state coupling under the frozen interaction rule**.
